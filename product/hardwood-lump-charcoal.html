Procurement FAQ

Hardwood Lump & Binchotan FAQ

Common questions from restaurant chains, charcoal importers, and catering distributors.

Halaban wood (Vitex pubescens) possesses a remarkably dense grain structure. When carbonized in traditional brick kilns, it produces rock-hard lump charcoal that burns with intense infrared heat, zero sparks, pleasant neutral aroma, and leaves pure white ash without tainting the delicate flavors of prime beef cuts.

Indonesian White Binchotan is produced through extreme high-temperature pyrolyzation (exceeding 1,000°C) followed by rapid smothering in a mixture of sand and ash. It produces metallic-ringing, ultra-pure carbon chunks (85-90%+ fixed carbon) that emit virtually zero smoke, flame, or odor, burning for up to 6 hours.
