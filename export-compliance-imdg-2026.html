Maritime Law Updates

What Changed in 2026 for Global Charcoal Importers?

Understanding the IMO IMDG Code Amendment 42-24 and why rogue suppliers using fake SHT non-DG certificates risk container confiscation at destination ports.

Critical Enforcement #1

Permanent Abolishment of SP925 (SHT Exemption)

Historically, brokers used Self-Heating Test (SHT) sheets to declare charcoal as non-hazardous cargo. Amendment 42-24 permanently repeals SP925. Shipping lines now reject non-DG bookings outright.

  • Previous Loophole: Non-DG booking via cheap SHT lab sheets.
  • Current Law: 100% UN 1361 Class 4.2 Dangerous Goods booking mandatory.
Critical Enforcement #2

Special Provision 978 (SP978 Headspace Rule)

All closed containers must maintain a minimum 30 cm clear airspace between the topmost cargo cartons and the ceiling of the container to enable convective heat dissipation.

  • Max Temperature: Cargo temperature verified ≤ 40°C prior to container sealing.
  • Loading Limits: Max 18 MT in 20ft FCL / 26 MT in 40ft HC with verified headspace.
Critical Enforcement #3

Mandatory Weathering & Aeration Timeline

Freshly extruded and baked briquettes must undergo a verified cooling and aeration timeline in certified chambers to dissipate internal kinetic heat before container stuffing.

  • Official Certificate: Signed Weathering Certificate by registered factory safety director.
  • Mandatory Carrier Check: Verified by Maersk, MSC, Hapag-Lloyd, and CMA CGM.
